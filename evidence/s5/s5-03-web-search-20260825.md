## Implemented boundary

- Application-owned policy maps exact HTTPS domains and approved subdomains to government,
  standards-body, vendor, or primary-research source classes.
- URL credentials, literal IP addresses, unsafe schemes, non-default ports, fragments, control
  characters, unapproved domains, and off-policy redirects fail closed.
- Active limits default to two queries and four pages and cannot exceed four-query and eight-page
  hard limits. Query and provider-page counts remain separate.
- Request queries and domains are normalized and deduplicated and cannot expand the application
  source policy. Duplicate pages do not create duplicate evidence.
- Current requests bypass and do not populate cache, require a dated fresh page, and report cache,
  provider, query, and page state separately.
- Non-current retrieval-cache keys bind exact tenant, project, user, role, permission, request,
  source domains, policy, provider, schema, and active-limit versions; refresh semantics prevent a
  stale version manifest from being reused.
- Bounded excerpts remain explicitly untrusted candidate evidence. Instruction-like page content
  is marked but never treated as an instruction or publication authority.
- Every accepted page has exact URL, title, source class, publication and access times, locator,
  content and excerpt hashes, evidence hash, and citation hash.
- Offline, provider, malformed, freshness, budget, and source failures are typed and cannot produce
  fabricated citations.
- The Tool Registry remains authoritative for permission, network, destination, timeout, retry,
  concurrency, physical tool-call budget, result validation, and hash-only audit. Cache hits avoid
  provider and network calls but still traverse and meter one registry invocation.
